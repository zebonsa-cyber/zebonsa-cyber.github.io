Address Report

    See an address, and everything around it, before you sign.

    Type a US address or click the map. You get the building from above, its flood zone, natural hazards, traffic noise, what's within walking distance, nearby schools, pollution sites and the neighborhood, all from public records.

    Try one of these:

    • 1001 Ocean DriveMiami Beach, Florida
    • 4801 Wilshire BoulevardLos Angeles, California
    • 1 Canal StreetNew Orleans, Louisiana
    • 100 West Main StreetBozeman, Montana

    Nothing you type is stored. Each lookup goes straight from your browser to FEMA, the Census Bureau, EPA, NCES, USGS, NOAA and OpenStreetMap.

    What this can't tell you

    Lot lines. Parcel boundaries come from county assessors and aren't in a free national dataset; the outline shown is the building, not the lot.

    Crime. There's no reliable free address-level crime data. Use the local police department's crime map.

    Exact internet providers. Check the FCC National Broadband Map for providers and speeds at this address.

    Assigned schools and the building's condition. Confirm attendance zones with the district, and get an inspection for radon, lead, mold and structure.

    This is a screening tool built from public datasets of different dates and resolutions. It is not an inspection, an appraisal, a flood determination, or legal or insurance advice. Neighborhood figures describe the Census tract, not the address.
